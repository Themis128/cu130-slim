## The offer (working name)

**The Full Digital Department for SMBs** — one partner for IT support,
branding, web presence, and e-shop. B2B only, deliberately.

## Required structure (every offer surface)

1. **Outcome** — a complete, *owned* digital operation: coherent brand, fast
   site, live e-shop taking orders, IT answered same day. One partner, one
   invoice, one phone call.
2. **For whom** — Greek SMBs (~2–50 ppl) dependent on tech without in-house
   IT; retailers needing a real e-shop; firms whose site understates their
   quality; owners tired of coordinating 3 vendors for 1 change.
3. **NOT for** — cheapest-landing-page shoppers; companies with real in-house
   IT/dev; DIY-ers; anyone unwilling to commit upfront (Code #3). Naming the
   "not for" raises perceived value — never skip it.
4. **Why you** — the problem: missing/fragmented presence quietly costs
   sales, downtime, and owner-hours babysitting vendors.
5. **Flex / why me** — proof only, real numbers (Code #6): full-stack under
   one roof; we run our own business on the stack we sell; showable outcomes
   (e.g. session polls 200s→10ms, 24/7 audited AI pipelines); in Greece,
   reachable when it breaks.
6. **Why now** — honest urgency: each month without a working e-shop is
   orders lost to whoever has one; patchwork debt compounds.
7. **Seasonality (optional but real)** — e.g. late-Oct window: e-shops that
   win Black Friday/Christmas go live in November, not December; miss it and
   the next organic season is Easter.
8. **The After** — transformation, not features: they own their stack
   (no lock-in), orders come in overnight, one call fixes IT, brand matches
   real quality. Owner-of-business, not owner-of-IT-problems.

## How prospects see it (perception map — answer these)

B2B buyers compare us against **hire in-house / agency / keep patching** —
not other freelancers.

- Works for us: one accountable partner; upfront pay = seriousness; a
  practitioner running the stack he sells.
- Risks to answer: breadth reads as jack-of-all-trades → package into named
  deliverables (audit → build → retainer), never a skill pile; 24/7
  skepticism → define the SLA (what's emergency, what automation covers);
  unknown price → anchor value before they ask.

## YES-Y mapping (which leg each surface fills)

- YES to YOU → proof posts, case studies (Authority leg).
- YES to the SOLUTION → problem/outcome content, the free audit.
- YES to the METHOD → how-it-works, "we run what we sell" mechanism posts.
- YES to the RATE → offer/proof/fit content + the survey's price question.

Surfaces: `cloudless.gr` service/pricing pages, LinkedIn Company Page offer
posts + the audit CTA (`https://cloudless.gr/contact`), email campaigns
(`email-campaigns-ops` — `offer_stack` in `voice_signature` is this same
H.O.T. model), proposals.

## Rules

- Every claim = a real outcome from this stack. If it can't be shown, cut it.
- "Not for" and upfront-commitment lines stay — they are Code #3 filtering.
- Seasonality must be true *this month* — regenerate it, never recycle stale
  urgency.
